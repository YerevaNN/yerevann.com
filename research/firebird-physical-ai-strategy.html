Decision framing

What Should Firebird Enable?

Four goals for an Armenian research program in small foundation models and Physical AI
1

Globally competitive research

Organize researchers from YerevaNN, CAST, and other Armenian teams around work that is scientifically useful beyond Armenia. Involve international advisors including Andre Martins, Michal Valko, Armen Aghajanyan, and Hrayr Harutyunyan.

2

Use Firebird at its highest value

Turn access to approximately 100 B200 GPUs into a durable research capability: ambitious final runs, disciplined scaling experiments, shared infrastructure, and repeated releases rather than idle capacity or disconnected projects.

3

Guarantee a small open foundation

Ensure that academics retain access to frontier-level 1-2B models, independent of future product and release decisions by Alibaba or any other single frontier laboratory.

4

Lead small Physical AI foundations

Develop the best foundation models for Physical AI at 4B parameters or below: perception, uncommon sensors, temporal understanding, world-action learning, and high-level control.

The central choice

Goal 3 vs. Goal 4

They are not logically incompatible, but they optimize different outcomes under fixed people, time, and compute.
Goal 3

Independent general foundation

  • Spend: general text, multilinguality, code, math, and reasoning
  • Must beat: the strongest current open 1-2B base
  • Payoff: supplier independence and tokenizer control
ComputeGeneric trillions of text tokens versus physical multimodal and action training
QualityBest inherited base versus independence from the supplier of that base
OrganizationGeneral foundation-model lab versus focused Physical AI program
Goal 4

Differentiated Physical AI foundation

  • Spend: sensors, video, simulation, state, and action
  • Must beat: current physical perception and control baselines
  • Payoff: differentiated science and deployable capability

Hypothesis to validate

Goal 3: Reasons for Skepticism

These are arguments to test with advisors, not a settled decision.
  • Compute does not reproduce the training systemAlibaba's advantage includes private data, filtering, synthetic-data generation, curriculum, teachers, and accumulated training expertise. A weaker base is not rescued by ordinary downstream fine-tuning.
  • "Always" requires a permanent institutionA one-time Firebird run is not continuity. The promise requires recurring data refreshes, experiments, evaluations, maintenance, and releases as the external frontier moves.
  • External releases cut both waysNew in 2026Liquid LFM2.5 1.2B (Jan 5); Cohere Tiny Aya 3.35B (Feb 17); Google Gemma 4 E2B (Apr 2); NVIDIA Cosmos3-Edge 4B (Jul 20); Cohere North Micro Vision 2.4B (Aug 12). Only Liquid directly matches a general 1-2B base.No relevant 2026 successor yetAi2 OLMo 2 1B (May 1, 2025); Hugging Face SmolLM3 3B (Jul 8, 2025); Mistral's Ministral 3B (Dec 2, 2025). This supports the concern that some labs may leave the category.

Why keep Goal 3 alive?

Armenian and underserved-language tokenizer: vocabulary design is difficult to change cleanly after full pretraining.

Strategic control: an owned lineage allows physical modalities and representations to enter pretraining rather than being attached later.

Decision test: is the unmet need specifically a continuously refreshed 1-2B general base? Most 2026 releases miss that exact target.

What Could Be Genuinely Better?

The target is a better Physical AI foundation, not a weaker general chatbot with a newer architecture.
Dimension
Specific program
Why it belongs before narrow fine-tuning
Physical data substrate
Synchronized RGB, radar, LiDAR, SAR, and multispectral streams linked to calibration, state, action, failures, technical video, robot code, and simulated episodes.
Creates cross-sensor and physical-sequence representations that RGB-text models do not supply.
Causal objectives
Future-latent prediction, inverse dynamics, action-conditioned futures, cross-modal prediction, sensor dropout, and sim-real invariance.
Teaches what changes because of an action, not only how a scene is described.
Action interface
A shared high-level action representation across UAVs and humanoids, while platform controllers retain low-level stabilization.
Targets the transferable AI layer that YerevaNN intends to own.
Tokenizer + representation
Efficient Armenian and underserved scripts, plus physical symbols, structured actions, coordinates, and telemetry.
Vocabulary and embedding allocation are difficult to repair after full pretraining; this is a concrete argument for owning the base.
Training method: multi-teacher distillation throughout base, multimodal, world-model, and policy training.
Public outputs: intermediate checkpoints, data manifests, scaling curves, sensor interfaces, and evaluations.
Success criterion: dominate the ≤4B Physical AI Pareto frontier while retaining sufficient general language and reasoning capability for downstream research.

What Would Actually Help?

Questions for the academic community and for Perceptron AI

Objective A: extend the best base

  1. Isaac 0.1 began from Qwen3-1.7B and SigLIP 2. What pretraining did you wish those upstream models had?
  2. Where should Armenia complement rather than duplicate Isaac, and which Firebird-scale dataset, encoder, model, or evaluation would Perceptron itself use?
  3. What can a 1-4B model reliably own in robotics: grounded perception, short planning, tool use, high-level closed-loop action, or something narrower?

Objective B: independent physical-native base

  1. What capability did you wish Qwen had learned during pretraining, and what physical data or objective could create it from scratch?
  2. What result at 100-500B pilot-token scale would justify a full run, and how should distillation be used in that pilot?
  3. Is tokenizer control, including Armenian and other underserved scripts, important enough to justify scratch training? What lower-cost alternatives should be tested first?
Desired outcome: identify one missing foundation artifact that Perceptron and academic teams would use, and agree on the experimental gate before committing Firebird-scale compute.
