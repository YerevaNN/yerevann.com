Decision framing

What Should Firebird Enable?

Four goals for an Armenian research program in small foundation models and Physical AI
1

Globally competitive research

Organize researchers from YerevaNN, CAST, and other Armenian teams around work that is scientifically useful beyond Armenia. Involve international advisors including Andre Martins, Michal Valko, Armen Aghajanyan, and Hrayr Harutyunyan.

2

Use Firebird at its highest value

Turn access to approximately 100 B200 GPUs into a durable research capability: ambitious final runs, disciplined scaling experiments, shared infrastructure, and repeated releases rather than idle capacity or disconnected projects.

3

Guarantee a small open foundation

Ensure that academics retain access to frontier-level 1-2B models, independent of future product and release decisions by Alibaba or any other single frontier laboratory.

4

Lead small Physical AI foundations

Develop the best foundation models for Physical AI at 4B parameters or below: perception, uncommon sensors, temporal understanding, world-action learning, and high-level control.

Goals 1 and 2 support either strategy. The decision is whether Goal 3 is a prerequisite for Goal 4, a parallel public-good program, or an expensive distraction from it.

The central choice

Goals 3 and 4 Compete for the Same Program

They are not logically incompatible, but they optimize different outcomes under fixed people, time, and compute.
Goal 3

Independent general foundation

  • Scope: language, knowledge, reasoning, coding, multilinguality
  • Winning move: reproduce frontier data quality and refresh continuously
  • Success: researchers choose it over an aging Qwen checkpoint
  • Primary value: continuity, openness, tokenizer and architecture control
ComputeGeneric trillions of text tokens versus physical multimodal and action training
QualityBest inherited base versus independence from the supplier of that base
OrganizationGeneral foundation-model lab versus focused Physical AI program
Possible resolution:
Stage the goals. Lead Physical AI now; earn the right to train an independent base through scaling evidence.
Goal 4

Differentiated Physical AI foundation

  • Scope: sensors, geometry, video, state, action, simulation
  • Winning move: start from the strongest base and add unique physical data
  • Success: academics and companies adopt it for real physical tasks
  • Primary value: scientific leadership and deployable capability

Hypothesis to validate

Goal 3: Reasons for Skepticism

These are arguments to test with advisors, not a settled decision.
  • The data advantage is privateQwen's architecture is partly disclosed; its filtering, synthetic-data, curriculum, and quality pipeline are not reproducible.
  • Architecture is not enoughA newer Kimi-like architecture trained on weaker public data may be faster yet still lose to Qwen3.5. Missing base capabilities are rarely recovered through ordinary post-training.
  • "Frontier" may be the wrong targetIf it includes robust agentic behavior, 1-2B may simply be below viable scale. The required capability set must be explicit.
  • "Always" is an institution, not a runIt requires recurring training, evaluation, maintenance, and releases while the external frontier keeps moving.
  • Compute is not the only bottleneck100 B200s can execute major runs, but do not automatically create Alibaba-scale data curation, teachers, or training expertise.
  • There is an opportunity costEvery generic-text experiment displaces sensor, video, simulation, world-action, and deployment work where Armenia could differentiate.

Why keep Goal 3 alive?

Armenian and underserved-language tokenizer: vocabulary design is difficult to change cleanly after full pretraining.

Public infrastructure: base weights, intermediate checkpoints, data manifests, and recipes have value even below absolute frontier quality.

Distillation at every stage: strong teachers may reduce the data-quality gap in pretraining, multimodal alignment, and post-training.

Strategic continuity: an independent lineage reduces dependence on the release policy of one company.

Decision test: can we identify a data or training advantage strong enough that an external practitioner would prefer our 1-2B base over Qwen3.5?

Path A to Goal 4

Extend the Strongest Available Base

Optimize first for globally competitive Physical AI research, accepting an upstream dependency.
Objective A

Best-base adaptation

Use Qwen3.5, Isaac, Cosmos Edge, or the strongest suitable successor.

Where Armenia differentiates

Physical data and objectives

Non-RGB sensing, temporal causality, simulation, action, and deployment feedback.

Frontier open VLMRetain language, reasoning, code, and RGB competence
Sensor encodersRadar, LiDAR, SAR, multispectral, cross-sensor alignment
Physical CPTVideo, simulation, state-action trajectories, future prediction
Open ≤4B foundationHigh-level UAV and humanoid perception and action

Why this can win

  • Starts from the highest available general capability.
  • Concentrates Firebird compute on differentiated physical learning.
  • Produces useful datasets, encoders, evaluations, and models sooner.
  • Allows new upstream bases to be swapped in as the frontier changes.
  • Fits YerevaNN's radar, multispectral, sim-to-real, and UAV work.

Overlap and complementarity

  • Perceptron Isaac: compact RGB perception, grounding, OCR, spatial reasoning.
  • NVIDIA Cosmos: physical reasoning, video/world generation, actions.
  • Armenian opportunity: uncommon sensors, synchronized cross-sensor data, sim-to-real, cross-embodiment high-level control.
Isaac 0.1 itself follows Path A: Qwen3-1.7B + SigLIP 2, then perceptive-language specialization. Repeating its RGB scope would duplicate Armen's work; extending the physical stack could complement it.

Path B to Goal 4

Build an Independent Physical-Native Base

Pursue independence only through a model whose pretraining is already shaped by Physical AI.
Objective B

Independent model flow

Own architecture, tokenizer, weights, physical data mixture, checkpoints, and release recipe.

Critical correction

Not 18T generic text first

General competence remains necessary, but differentiation must enter pretraining early.

Architecture + tokenizerEdge-efficient sequence model; Armenian and underserved scripts
Curated base + distillationCompact high-quality text, code, science, spatial and procedural data
Native physical pretrainingImages, video, sensors, geometry, state, action, simulated interventions
Independent ≤4B baseOpen model flow designed for continued physical adaptation

What this buys

  • Control over immutable choices: tokenizer, modality interfaces, context structure.
  • A durable lineage not tied to Alibaba's future release sizes or licenses.
  • Early fusion of uncommon sensors and physical sequences.
  • Scientific access to every checkpoint, mixture, ablation, and failure.
  • A model that can serve Goal 3 partially while being judged on Goal 4.

What could make it fail

  • A weak general base remains worse than Qwen after physical post-training.
  • Early multimodal training can consume compute without useful sensor-language grounding.
  • Physical datasets may be too small, narrow, or poorly synchronized.
  • Distillation can import teacher capability, but does not create true future independence.
  • The program can become too broad for Armenia's research capacity.
Scale gate: do not approve a full multi-trillion-token run until 100-500B-token pilots show a credible parity trend against Qwen3.5 and a measurable Physical AI advantage.

What Could Be Genuinely Better?

The target is a better Physical AI foundation, not a weaker general chatbot with a newer architecture.
Dimension
Specific program
Why it belongs before narrow fine-tuning
Physical corpus
Robot manuals, ROS/PX4/URDF code, spatial and procedural text, technical video, simulated episodes, failures, and calibrated sensor logs.
Builds vocabulary and representations around physical state, procedure, uncertainty, and intervention.
Native modalities
Synchronized RGB + radar + LiDAR + SAR + multispectral streams, with calibration and geometry metadata.
Creates cross-sensor representations that RGB-only frontier models do not supply.
Causal objectives
Future-latent prediction, inverse dynamics, action-conditioned futures, cross-modal prediction, sensor dropout, and sim-real invariance.
Teaches what changes because of an action, not only how a scene is described.
Embodiment
A shared high-level action representation across UAVs and humanoids, while platform controllers retain low-level stabilization.
Targets the transferable AI layer that YerevaNN intends to own.
Tokenizer
Efficient Armenian and underserved-script tokenization, plus physical symbols, structured actions, coordinates, and telemetry.
Vocabulary and embedding allocation are awkward to repair after serious pretraining; this is one real argument for scratch training.
Distillation
Use multiple teachers during base, multimodal, reasoning, world-model, and policy stages; distill representations as well as outputs.
May narrow the private-data quality gap while reducing dependence on any single teacher.
Open science
Release intermediate checkpoints, data manifests, scaling curves, ablations, sensor interfaces, and evaluation suites.
Can be globally valuable even when generic benchmark parity is incomplete.
Success criterion: dominate the ≤4B Physical AI Pareto frontier while retaining sufficient general language and reasoning capability for downstream research.

What Would Actually Help?

Questions for the academic community and for Perceptron AI

Objective A: extend the best base

  1. Isaac 0.1 began from Qwen3-1.7B and SigLIP 2. What pretraining did you wish those upstream models had?
  2. Where should Armenia complement rather than duplicate Isaac: non-RGB sensing, video and temporal learning, simulation, world-action modeling, or high-level control?
  3. Which Firebird-scale public asset would Perceptron itself use: an open dataset, sensor encoder, continued-pretrained VLM, simulator corpus, action representation, or evaluation suite?
  4. What can a 1-4B model reliably own in robotics: grounded perception, short planning, tool use, high-level closed-loop action, or something narrower?

Objective B: independent physical-native base

  1. What physical pretraining data or objective could make scratch training better than continued training from Qwen3.5?
  2. What result at 100-500B pilot-token scale would justify a full multi-trillion-token run?
  3. How would you use distillation in each stage, and can multiple teachers provide independence rather than simply reproduce a new dependency?
  4. Is tokenizer control, including Armenian and other underserved scripts, important enough to justify scratch training? What lower-cost alternatives should be tested first?
Desired outcome: choose Path A, Path B, or a staged A → B strategy; identify one missing foundation artifact; define who would use it; and agree on the experimental gate before committing Firebird-scale compute.
