How to read the roadmap

The program starts by building an end-to-end Physical AI capability on a programmable UAV, transfers the reusable AI layers to an internationally recognized humanoid, and then generalizes them to Armenian humanoid hardware and sensing.

The five-layer system

Sensors provide observations to sensor-aligned perception models. Perception feeds the high-level policy, which selects actions at approximately the level of a human operator. Those commands are executed through low-level controllers on the physical hardware. The grid is a capability map rather than a single downward pipeline: sensing flows upward, while actions flow downward.

The exact interface between high-level policy and low-level control remains an open design question; the appropriate action abstraction may differ across platforms.

Year 1–2: a provisional UAV platform

The current leading choice is the Holybro X500 V2 with Pixhawk and PX4, but procurement remains subject to a focused validation. The selection criteria are: physical use in strong robotics or vision research; programmable high-level control; synchronized observation, action and flight logging; payload capacity for RGB and multispectral sensing; simulator and hardware-in-the-loop support; extensibility; research availability; and stable replacement parts.

The X500 satisfies several of these criteria: Holybro publishes a modular development kit with payload mounting and PX4 integration; PX4 exposes offboard setpoints, ROS 2 connectivity, detailed ULog recording, and Gazebo simulation. Physical X500-family systems have been used for autonomous data collection in ICRA 2023, payload-intensive contact experiments in ICRA 2024, and camera-data acquisition in the NeurIPS 2025 InFlux benchmark. See also the X500 V2 kit, PX4 offboard control, logging, and simulation documentation.

The remaining validation should test the exact multispectral payload, onboard compute, storage, power, flight duration, outdoor safety procedures, and whether the platform can produce the complete observation-action datasets needed by the policy work.

Holybro would serve as the internationally recognized reference platform. In parallel, the program will look for a suitable Armenian UAV that can be used alongside it. An Armenian platform should be added when it provides comparable programmability, synchronized data and action access, sensor extensibility, research availability, and a credible simulation path. Using both would support cross-platform generalization while also advancing local hardware.

UAV intelligence

RGB perception starts from established encoders such as DINOv3 or CLIP. YerevaNN's χViT line targets efficient multispectral perception. Above perception, YerevaNN develops navigation and active-search policies. Active search means selecting flight actions that reduce uncertainty, locate a target, or acquire a more useful viewpoint rather than following a fixed route.

Ground2Sat, the satellite-to-ground matching line, is an enabling component rather than a separate policy cell. Its intended role is to match oblique UAV or ground observations against satellite imagery, providing geographic evidence to navigation and helping an active-search policy reason about what has been observed and where to move next.

Year 2+: Agile ONE S

Agile ONE S is the internationally recognized humanoid reference. The public sensor description confirms built-in cameras and LiDAR, but does not disclose camera specifications, the LiDAR perception model, raw-data interfaces, or the low-level whole-body controller. The slide therefore labels the controller as Agile proprietary controller and the LiDAR perception stack as undisclosed. DINOv3 or CLIP provides the initial visual encoder; YerevaNN's original work concentrates on policy adaptation, navigation, and industrial manipulation.

Year 3+: Armenian humanoid stack

CyberFusion provides the Armenian humanoid, its controller, and the ability to integrate selected sensors. Waveye supplies the Armenian radar, while Karen Eguizarian's lab is shown as a provisional path toward an additional Armenian sensor and a corresponding new vision model. YerevaNN develops the radar perception models. Together, these components create a controlled setting for studying transfer from Agile ONE S to a second humanoid and generalization across embodiments and sensors.
