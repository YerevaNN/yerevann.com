Physical AI research and deployment roadmap

UAVs will be the first platform on which we establish the complete Physical AI loop: real data collection → simulation → AI model training → deployment → new real data. The resulting models, infrastructure, and operating methods will transfer to CyberFusion Robot Dogs and to Agile and CyberFusion humanoids as those platforms become available. Each date in the diagram is a delivery deadline for an integrated capability; individual components follow their own schedules.

Research architecture

The program has five connected layers. Hardware provides the physical embodiment. Low-level control maintains stability and executes motion. Sensors produce observations for sensor-specific perception models. A high-level policy combines perception, instructions, and task context to select actions at approximately the level of a human operator. Memory, world state, reasoning, and task planning are included in this top layer.

The exact interface between high-level policy and low-level control remains an open research and design question. The appropriate action abstraction may differ across UAVs, quadrupeds, and humanoids. Existing controllers will initially execute these actions, while Astghik Hakobyan's lab may develop new control methods where the available controller is insufficient.

Work already underway

YerevaNN has already started two parts of the UAV intelligence stack. The navigation work currently trains and evaluates policies in simulation and is expected to transfer to a physical UAV as additional resources are allocated in the coming months. The multispectral research program, including χViT, is active across remote-sensing problems, with several papers already written; this work provides the basis for efficient multispectral perception on UAVs.

Radar data collection and model development have also started. TI provides the internationally available radar reference, and the plan to work with Waveye hardware has been agreed. YerevaNN will develop AI models for both sensor families and study generalization across radar manufacturers, configurations, and future hardware revisions.

Milestone 1 — delivery by end of 2027: UAV capability

The UAV milestone is successful when at least one physical UAV platform supports a real-data collection pipeline, a navigation policy, an active-search policy, and a functioning deployment feedback loop. The milestone does not require every candidate platform or sensor integration to be complete.

The current leading international platform candidate is the Holybro X500 V2 with Pixhawk and PX4. Final selection requires validation of programmability, synchronized observation and action logging, simulator support, research availability, payload capacity, onboard compute, power, storage, flight duration, and safe outdoor operation. The X500 already satisfies important criteria: PX4 provides offboard control, ROS 2 connectivity, ULog recording, and Gazebo simulation, while physical X500-family platforms have appeared in ICRA 2023, ICRA 2024, and the NeurIPS 2025 InFlux benchmark. Relevant technical references include the X500 V2 kit, PX4 offboard interface, logging system, and simulation stack.

Holybro would serve as the internationally recognized reference. The program will also identify an Armenian UAV that can be used alongside it if a local platform provides comparable programmability, data access, extensibility, availability, and simulation support. Operating both would create a direct research setting for cross-platform generalization.

Agentic UAV policies and specialized tools

The UAVs will be governed by agentic VLM or VLA policies for navigation and active search. These policies will not need to learn every capability inside one monolithic model: they will be able to call specialized perception tools. Ground2Sat will match oblique UAV observations against satellite imagery to support geographic localization and map-grounded navigation. Object re-identification models will help maintain object identity across satellite, aerial, and ground viewpoints. Active search will use these tools to select movements that reduce uncertainty, find targets, or acquire more informative views.

CyberFusion Robot Dog — available from November 2026

CyberFusion Robot Dogs will provide the first Armenian robotic platform for transferring and testing the stack developed for UAVs. Initial work will focus on navigation and on reproducing the complete data–simulation–training–deployment loop. CyberFusion will provide an Isaac Sim environment, built-in RGB cameras, LiDAR, and an IMU. With CyberFusion's engineering support, TI and Waveye radars can be added to the platform.

The Robot Dog is an interim embodiment, but the work is directly relevant to future CyberFusion products: almost the entire software stack, including data collection, simulation, sensor integration, perception, policy training, deployment infrastructure, and evaluation, is expected to carry forward to the CyberFusion Humanoid.

Milestone 2 — delivery by end of 2028: humanoid capability

The humanoid milestone is successful when the integrated AI stack operates on at least one available humanoid. Agile ONE S is expected approximately in early 2027, while the CyberFusion Humanoid is expected approximately in early 2028; these dates remain provisional. High-level humanoid policy work will begin when the robots become available, building on the methods and infrastructure established with UAVs and the CyberFusion Robot Dog.

Agile ONE S is the internationally recognized reference platform. Its public description confirms built-in cameras and LiDAR, but access to raw sensor streams, logging, simulation assets, and control interfaces remains to be established. CyberFusion Humanoid is the Armenian platform and is expected to support deeper customization, including additional sensors. The two target high-level capabilities are navigation and industrial manipulation; the first concrete industrial task and benchmark remain to be selected.

Waveye radar and a possible new sensor from Karen Eguizarian's lab create opportunities for perception beyond standard RGB. Karen's lab may contribute the sensor, signal processing, and potentially its AI model. YerevaNN will develop the Waveye and TI radar models and study cross-radar transfer. Standard cameras can initially use established vision encoders such as DINOv3 or CLIP.

Open-weight model dependency

Most high-level policies will adapt the strongest suitable open-weight VLM or VLA available at the time, with a target size of at most 4B parameters for practical experimentation and deployment. Qwen3.5 is the current reference. This creates a strategic dependency: frontier laboratories may stop releasing competitive open-weight models in this size range. Physical AI research teams would then be forced to keep adapting aging foundations, while important capabilities missing from a base model generally cannot be recovered through ordinary fine-tuning. Maintaining a strong small open-weight foundation may therefore become an additional infrastructure project for this program and the wider research community.

Armenian ecosystem roles

YerevaNN develops the high-level policies and the specialized perception models marked with its logo. CyberFusion provides Armenian robot hardware, controllers, simulation, and sensor integration. Waveye provides radar hardware. Karen Eguizarian's lab may contribute a new sensor and its processing stack. Astghik Hakobyan's lab may address low-level control problems when existing controllers do not provide the required behavior. The Armenian markers in the diagram identify these local contributions and the areas where internationally recognized platforms are paired with Armenian technology.
